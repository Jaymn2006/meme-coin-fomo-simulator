Market cap

Price multiplied by supply. A bigger number does not automatically mean a healthier project.

Liquidity

Available trading depth. Low liquidity can make buying or selling move the price sharply.

Slippage

The gap between the price you expect and the price you receive when the market moves.

Bonding curve

A programmed pricing curve where each buy pushes the next token price higher.

Crypto learning lab

Use this page to understand the machinery behind the chart. The simulator deliberately includes uncertainty because real markets are adaptive systems, not answer keys.

Probability, not certainty

Each setup is a distribution of possible outcomes. A good risk process can still lose on one trade. Think in expected value and position sizing, never in guaranteed calls.

E[X] = Σ pᵢ · returnᵢ

Returns and volatility

Prices compound multiplicatively. Log return compares proportional movement, while volatility measures dispersion around that movement.

rₜ = ln(Pₜ / Pₜ₋₁)

Calculus of momentum

The slope of the price curve is momentum. A changing slope is acceleration, but both can reverse when liquidity or sentiment changes.

momentum ≈ dP/dt · acceleration ≈ d²P/dt²

Liquidity and impact

A market order consumes available depth. Thin books create nonlinear impact, so the displayed price is not always the executed price.

impact ∝ size / depth

Bayesian updating

New evidence should update a belief, not confirm a story. A headline, volume spike, or whale move can change probabilities without proving direction.

P(H|D) ∝ P(D|H) · P(H)

Regimes and feedback

Markets switch between trend, chop, panic, and recovery. Traders react to price, and that reaction changes the next price, creating feedback and reflexivity.

P(stateₜ | stateₜ₋₁) ≠ constant
Prediction limit: randomness, hidden information, adversarial behavior, fees, and regime changes mean even sophisticated analysis can be wrong. Practice process, drawdown control, and exit planning rather than trying to make the future certain.
